02 / The base repository

The repository is a small, staged research pipeline.

Why group by patient? If slices from one patient were split across training and validation, the model could benefit from patient-specific visual cues. The frozen split assigns the patient first; only then are slices assigned to train or validation.

04 / The model mechanism

The CNN sees slices; the study is judged on patients.

The model produces a Stone probability for each original slice. Those probabilities are averaged within a patient. The patient score is then ranked against the recorded patient label.

Input
384 × 384 grayscale CT slice.
Feature extraction
Four convolutional blocks with 64, 128, 256, and 512 filters. Each block uses two convolutions, BatchNorm, ReLU, and 2×2 pooling.
Classification head
Global average pooling → Dense(192) → ReLU → Dropout(0.35) → two-class softmax.
Size
Approximately 4,788,866 total parameters; approximately 4,785,026 trainable parameters.
Training recipe for E023
AdamW, learning rate 0.0001, weight decay 0.0001, batch size 8, label smoothing 0.05, mild online augmentation, maximum 60 epochs.

08 / What the experiments taught us

The biggest gains came from fixing evidence quality.

The research did not simply search for a larger score. It found which measurements could be trusted, which training signals were misleading, and where the final training protocol stopped transferring.

Data order

The old 1,024-item shuffle buffer interacted badly with a class-sorted manifest. Batches became class-biased and folds collapsed. Full-dataset shuffling corrected the mechanism and produced E000.

Derivatives

Training with supplied derivatives caused memorization and validation collapse. The accepted configuration keeps derivatives disabled.

Patient unit

Negative patients average 18.89 slices and positive patients 15.74. The top 10% of patients contribute 28.25% of slices. Slice count is therefore not the same as independent sample count.

Checkpointing

Seed-44 diagnostics showed that selecting checkpoints by patient validation AUC rescued weak folds. The aligned full seed-43 run E023 then reached a strong and stable five-fold result.

Transfer

Several candidates had higher OOF AUC but their score distributions changed outside the held-out folds. E005 and E006 collapsed in opposite directions; E007/E008 fixed spread but not distribution shape; E015/E020/E024 were transfer near-misses.

External data

KidneyStoneTR was quarantined because its archive lacked a verifiable patient-to-slice map and its advertised holdout contained duplicate images across directories. It was not used to inflate training or validation.

Correct interpretation: E023 is a verified development candidate under the project’s gates. It is not a medical-grade model, a clinical validation study, or evidence that the score will transfer to another hospital.
09 / Inference

Inference requires the patient grouping to survive the whole path.

A single slice can receive a probability, but the project’s primary output is one score per patient. The accepted E023 evidence uses the five fold-best checkpoints as an equal-mean ensemble.

What an inference wrapper must do

  • Match preprocessing384×384 grayscale, with the same normalization used by the training pipeline.
  • Load all five E023 checkpointsAverage their slice probabilities before the patient aggregation.
  • Keep patient IDsGroup all slices belonging to a patient before returning the main result.
  • Report provenanceRecord model paths, threshold, configuration, and the research-only status.

What is not available

  • No accepted sealed-test scoreThe sealed test is permanently excluded from tuning and must not be reopened.
  • No clinical deployment claimThe project has no prospective study, external hospital cohort, or medical certification.
  • No safe single-image conclusionThe primary metric and threshold are patient-level.
10 / Limits and next goals

The bottleneck is trustworthy generalization, not just model size.

The current candidate has cleared the documented development bar. The next improvement must come from stronger evidence and better transfer, not from reopening a protected test set.

What limits the current conclusion

Small patient count201 patients total; 161 development patients.
Related slices3,364 original images are not 3,364 independent cases.
Uneven contributionPatients contribute different numbers of slices; the largest 10% contribute 28.25% of slices.
Site confoundingDevelopment AUC varies by FA, ME, and RA; site-only baseline is about 0.562.
External cohort gapNo verified independent patient-mapped cohort is available in the current study.
Sealed boundaryThe 40-patient sealed set cannot be used for additional tuning.

Responsible next steps

  1. Obtain patient-mapped external dataRequire acquisition, label, and provenance documentation before treating it as independent evidence.
  2. Investigate transfer stabilityStudy the BatchNorm/final-training score-regime mismatch without touching the sealed set.
  3. Improve inference packagingMake patient grouping, five-checkpoint loading, thresholding, and provenance explicit.
  4. Report uncertaintyUse patient-level confidence intervals and subgroup counts when an independent cohort exists.

Good enough means “good enough for this research stage.” E023 is repeat-confirmed, transfer-safe within the development guard, reproducible, and verified. That is a meaningful project result; it is not the end of clinical validation.
